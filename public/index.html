Case study A

Wholesale distributor moves to WooCommerce with QuickBooks Desktop sync

Result: 2,400+ products live with three dealer pricing tiers, and 99% of products matched to QuickBooks automatically.

The client

A B2B wholesale distributor of hot tub and pool parts, selling to dealers, technicians, and contractors. Leaving a legacy e-commerce platform, with accounting in QuickBooks Desktop running on a local office machine.

The problem

  • Product data lived in two places: prices in the QuickBooks price list, categories and images in the old platform's export. Some products existed in only one of them.
  • Pricing was complex: three customer tiers with different discounts, plus 93 products where the top tier pays a different rate.
  • Orders had to reach a QuickBooks Desktop install on a local PC, not a cloud system.

What I did

  1. Wrote a Python data merge that joined both sources on SKU, calculated every price tier, flagged the exception products, and produced review reports for missing prices and categories. The owner signed off on the data before anything was imported.
  2. Imported the full catalog with categories and images, and configured B2B tiered pricing with override rules for every exception. Prices are hidden from guests.
  3. Migrated the site to production hosting after the standard migration tool failed on size, compressing it by 65% with a different approach.
  4. Set up two-way QuickBooks Desktop sync. Orders, customers, and payments flow into QuickBooks as sales orders for staff review. Inventory flows back to the store hourly.
  5. Debugged the integration end to end: an outdated TLS certificate issue on the office PC, orders stuck in a status the sync ignored, and a sales tax code error that blocked orders from posting.

Outcome

  • 2,455 of 2,478 products matched automatically between the store and QuickBooks (99%+). The remaining 23 were discontinued or placeholder items.
  • All 93 pricing exceptions configured as specified.
  • Staff review every web order in QuickBooks instead of re-keying it.

Case study B

The order that disappeared without a trace

Result: Proved a real, paid customer order had been completely erased from a store's database, something the store's own records could not show.

The client

A hot tub retailer selling parts online through WooCommerce, with accounting in QuickBooks Online.

The problem

The owner noticed orders vanishing from the store while still showing up elsewhere. Separately, website sales had stopped reaching QuickBooks at all. Nobody could say what was missing or why.

What I did

  1. Mapped exactly where the store keeps its orders. Then wrote forensic database queries to profile every order status, list trashed orders, and sweep the whole store for paid orders that were no longer live.
  2. Found that most disappearing orders were card-testing fraud: bots placing tiny charges with throwaway emails, then being cleared in daily batches. Of 15 removed orders, 14 never took payment. I flagged the one that did so it could be refunded.
  3. Built a risk flag that marks any removed order as paid if it had payment evidence before it was deleted.
  4. Then the owner produced a supplier invoice for a real customer order that should have existed on the site. I searched every angle: customer name, address, phone, postal code, part number, user accounts. Nothing. The order, its details, and its line items were gone entirely.
  5. Documented everything in a handoff report and set up a new QuickBooks sync that only posts paid orders, so fraud never reaches the books.

Outcome

  • 15 removed orders analyzed and classified. Fraud separated from real sales.
  • One genuine paid order proven lost, exposing a blind spot that a database check alone can't catch.
  • Fraud kept out of accounting going forward.

The lesson for any store owner: if the order is deleted completely, your store won't tell you it's missing. The only way to catch it is to compare the store against outside records (payment processor, accounting, suppliers) on a schedule. That comparison is exactly what the monthly plan does.
